To understand Buffer Turnaround Management, you have to look at it as the "Insurance Policy" of an airline's schedule. In aviation, a "Turnaround" is the period an aircraft spends on the ground between landing and taking off again.
The Buffer is the deliberate "extra time" added to the minimum possible turnaround to absorb unexpected shocks.

1. The Core Definition
A Buffer is not "dead time"; it is Elastic Time.
* Minimum Turnaround Time (MTT): The fastest a plane can technically be serviced (e.g., 30 minutes for a Low-Cost Carrier).
* Scheduled Turnaround Time (STT): The time the airline puts in the public schedule (e.g., 45 minutes).
* The Buffer: The 15-minute difference ($STT - MTT = Buffer$).
2. The Four Pillars of Buffer Management
In your simulation, the "Management" part of this system comes from how you adjust this time based on four main categories of variables:
A. Environmental (The Weather Factor)
This is your primary focus. High temperatures cause "thermal throttling" of human and mechanical performance.
* Logic: When the heat index rises, the buffer must expand because the "work rate" (Baggage/Refueling) slows down.
B. Operational (The Resource Factor)
This involves the availability of Ground Support Equipment (GSE) and staff.
* Logic: If the airport is congested and a fuel truck is shared between three gates, the "waiting time" increases. The buffer accounts for this "Resource Contention."
C. Stochastic (The Random Factor)
These are "Acts of God" or random human errors.
* Logic: A passenger arriving late at the gate, a technical glitch in the cockpit, or a localized security alert. These are modeled using probability distributions (e.g., a 2% chance of a "No-show" passenger).
D. Technical (The Mechanical Factor)
The reliability of the aircraft and ground machines.
* Logic: If a belt loader breaks down, the "Buffer" is what prevents that breakdown from making the flight 30 minutes late.

4. The "Cascade" Effect (Why it matters)
The goal of Buffer Management is to prevent Delay Propagation.
* Without a buffer, a 10-minute delay on the first flight of the morning could grow into a 2-hour delay by the final flight of the night because the delays "stack" up.
* Your system identifies when to increase the buffer to "reset" the clock and stop the delay from spreading to the rest of the fleet.

5. Key Metrics for the Management Student
When you present this, don't just talk about "time." Talk about these Aviation KPIs:
* OTP (On-Time Performance): The % of flights departing within 15 mins of schedule.
* Ground Occupancy Cost: The fees paid to the airport for every minute the plane sits at the gate.
* Utilization Rate: How many hours a day the plane is actually flying vs. sitting.
* SLA Compliance: Whether the Ground Handler met the contract timing.

5. Detailed Logic Guidelines for the Expert
Ask the expert to implement the following Stochastic Distributions (Standard Aviation Math):
A. De-boarding & Boarding Logic
Don't use a flat number. Use a Triangular Distribution:
* De-boarding Time: triangular(10, 15, 25) minutes.
* Boarding Time: triangular(20, 30, 45) minutes.
* Why? Because some passengers are slower, some are faster.
B. The Heat-Efficiency Formula
The expert should write a small function in AnyLogic (Java-based):
Java
double efficiencyMultiplier = 1.0;
if (p_AmbientTemp > 40) {
    efficiencyMultiplier = 1.0 + (p_AmbientTemp - 40) * 0.04; // 4% delay per degree
}
if (p_Humidity > 70) {
    efficiencyMultiplier += 0.05; // Extra 5% delay for high humidity
}
This multiplier should be applied to the Delay blocks for Baggage Loading and Refueling.
C. The Cost Logic (The Business Value)
To calculate the "Money Making" part, use this formula in a permanent Sink or Statistics block:
* Total Cost of Delay: (Actual_Departure - Scheduled_Departure) * $75.00
* Note: Only calculate if Actual > Scheduled.

o When the Baggage_Loading block starts (On Enter), use a line of code like: tug.moveTo(aircraft.getPos());.
